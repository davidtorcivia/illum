# Seeds

Ideas that don't fit a research file yet. Collect, don't build. Each is one line of what, one of why it might be more than pretty.

## Ornament that reads the page
- **Hierarchy-grown borders.** The DOM's heading tree is the plant: h1 a trunk, h2 branches, h3 twigs, paragraphs leaves. The ornament *is* the table of contents; a long section literally grows a longer vine. Structure made visible, not decoration bolted on.
- **Marginalia that comment.** Books of Hours drolleries mocked or echoed the text beside them. Ornament keyed to words in the paragraph (a snail beside "slowly", a knot beside "therefore"). A small lexicon → motif table, sparse, one per screen at most.
- **Reading wear.** The page ages where *you* have read: paragraphs you lingered on get thumbed edges, faint foxing; unread sections stay crisp. Per-visitor patina in localStorage. The page remembers being a book.
- **Rubrication by meaning.** Medieval scribes put key words in red. Rubricate automatically: proper nouns, first occurrences of defined terms, the sentence a paragraph turns on.
- **Catchwords and signatures.** At the foot of each viewport-"leaf", the first word of the next one, as in quires. Scroll becomes gathering.

## Time
- **Growth as reading progress.** The margin garden grows as you scroll; reach the end and it's in bloom. Leave and return: a season has passed (date-seeded season).
- **Ink that is still wet.** New content (a fresh post, a live comment) arrives with wet ink: glossy, darker at the pooled edge, drying over minutes into its final matte tone.
- **Time of day lighting on gold.** Gilding's specular highlight follows the sun's real position for the visitor's location/time; at night the page is lit by a "candle" at the cursor.
- **Slow-loading as a virtue.** Instead of hiding render time, show the hand: the initial is drawn stroke by stroke in its actual stroke order, first time only.

## Material
- **Page as a real substrate.** One shared paper field (fibres, laid lines, sizing) under the whole site, so every stroke on every page interacts with the *same* grain: washes granulate into the same valleys. Continuity across pages = one book.
- **Pigment budgets.** A page has a limited "pot" of each pigment (lapis was expensive). Ornament spends it; the most important section gets the ultramarine. Scarcity as hierarchy.
- **Palimpsest.** Previous versions of the text (git history) faintly visible underneath, scraped away. The edit history as texture.
- **Gold that is actually reflective.** Device-orientation / pointer-driven specular on burnished gold vs flat shell gold; punched patterns catching light.
- **Two-sided leaves.** Strong ornament on the "recto" shows through faintly (mirrored) on the next section, as ink bleeds through thin paper.

## Type
- **Initials grown from the letter's own skeleton.** Medial axis of the glyph → stems; ornament fills counters and the square around. The letter is the trellis.
- **Ligature-ornament.** Penwork flourishes that leave from real stroke terminals of the actual font (needs glyph outlines and terminal detection).
- **Code illumination.** Code blocks as manuscripts: function names as historiated initials, nesting depth as border density, comments as marginal glosses in a smaller hand.
- **Numbers as ornament.** Folios, footnote markers, list counters drawn as tiny roundels; a footnote marker grows a thread to its note.

## Composition
- **The carpet page.** One full-viewport purely ornamental page between sections, built from the text that follows (its letters as tile seeds). A breath, Lindisfarne-style.
- **Ornament density ∝ importance, not area.** Invert the usual "fill the space": the most important thing gets the most ornament even if small, and empty regions stay empty.
- **Pattern that breaks once.** A border repeats faithfully for its whole length and errs once, deliberately (the Persian flaw / Navajo spirit line). The error is the signature, seed-placed.
- **Collaborative illumination.** Visitors each contribute one stroke (seeded from something about them); the border of a page is drawn by its readers over time.
- **Sound-drawn.** pie-site has audio of reconstructed speech; let the voice's pitch contour draw the rule under the line it speaks. Prosody as penwork.
